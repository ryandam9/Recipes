Banana Choc-Chip Cake

A soft banana cake made in the multicooker. Little sugar, no butter, and the bananas do the sweetening.

1 hr 30Total time
EasySkill level
3 tbspSugar in the whole cake
8Slices

Ingredients

Tick them off as you go.

Makes 1 cake, about 8 slices

Method

Every small step, in order. Read it through once before you start.

  1. 1

    Get everything ready

    • Take out 2 bowls: one big, one medium.
    • Get a fork, a whisk (or another fork), a big spoon or spatula, measuring cups and spoons.
    • Take the inner pot out of the multicooker.
    • Measure all the ingredients and put them on the bench. This makes the rest easy.

    Use bananas with lots of brown spots. Yellow bananas make a less sweet cake.

    5 minutes
  2. 2

    Grease the pot

    • Pour 1 tsp oil into the inner pot.
    • Rub it all over the bottom and the sides with a paper towel.
    • Add 1 spoon of plain flour.
    • Turn and tap the pot so the flour sticks to all the oily parts.
    • Turn the pot upside down over the sink and tap out the loose flour.

    The inside should look lightly dusted white. This stops the cake sticking.

    2 minutes
  3. 3

    Mash the bananas

    • Peel the 4 bananas and put them in the big bowl.
    • Press them flat with the back of a fork.
    • Keep mashing for about 1 minute.

    Looks right when it is like a thick, wet paste. Small lumps are fine.

    2 minutes
  4. 4

    Add the wet ingredients

    • Crack the 2 eggs into the banana bowl. Pick out any bits of shell.
    • Add ½ cup milk.
    • Add 3 tbsp olive oil.
    • Add 3 tbsp brown sugar (or honey).
    • Add 1 tsp vanilla, if you have it.
    • Whisk for 1 minute.

    Looks right when it is all one colour and you can't see streaks of egg.

    3 minutes
  5. 5

    Mix the dry ingredients

    • In the medium bowl, add ½ cup wholemeal flour and ½ cup plain flour.
    • Add 2 tsp baking powder, 1 tsp cinnamon and ¼ tsp salt.
    • Stir with a spoon for 30 seconds.

    Mixing these first spreads the baking powder evenly, so the whole cake rises.

    1 minute
  6. 6

    Combine the two bowls

    • Tip the dry bowl into the wet bowl.
    • Stir slowly with a big spoon. Scoop from the bottom and fold it over the top.
    • Stop as soon as you can't see any dry flour. This takes about 20 to 30 stirs.
    • Keep a small handful of chocolate chips aside. Add the rest and give 3 or 4 more stirs.

    Don't over-stir. A few lumps are good. Too much stirring makes the cake hard and flat.

    2 minutes
  7. 7

    Fill the pot

    • Pour the batter into the greased pot. Scrape the bowl clean with the spoon.
    • Smooth the top flat with the back of the spoon.
    • Sprinkle the chocolate chips you kept on top.
    • Lift the pot about 2 cm and drop it gently on the bench 2 or 3 times. This pops big air bubbles.
    • Wipe the outside and bottom of the pot dry.

    The batter fills about a third of the pot. That's normal. It rises as it cooks.

    2 minutes
  8. 8

    Start the multicooker

    • Put the pot into the multicooker and turn it a little so it sits flat.
    • Close the lid until it clicks.
    • Press Menu until Cake (or Bake) lights up.
    • Use the time buttons to set 50 minutes.
    • Press Start.
    • Don't open the lid for the first 40 minutes. Cold air makes the cake sink.

    Button names change a little between models. If yours has no Cake setting, use Rice/Cook. When it switches to Keep Warm, run it again.

    50 minutes
  9. 9

    Check if it's cooked

    • When it beeps, open the lid. Steam comes out, so keep your face back.
    • Push a skewer or thin knife into the middle, down to the bottom.
    • Pull it out and look at it.
    • Clean, or a few dry crumbs: it's done. Go to step 10.
    • Wet, sticky batter: close the lid, cook 10 more minutes on Cake, then check again.

    The top stays pale and a bit soft. That's normal in a multicooker. Go by the skewer, not the colour.

    0–10 minutes
  10. 10

    Let it cool in the pot

    • Switch the cooker off.
    • Use oven gloves to lift the hot pot out and put it on a board.
    • Leave it for 10 minutes. Don't touch it.

    A hot cake is soft and breaks easily. Cooling firms it up.

    10 minutes
  11. 11

    Turn it out

    • Run a butter knife all the way around the edge of the cake.
    • Put a big plate upside down on top of the pot.
    • With oven gloves on, hold the plate and the pot together tightly.
    • Flip them over quickly.
    • Lift the pot off. The cake should drop onto the plate. If it sticks, tap the bottom of the pot a few times.

    The golden bottom is now on top. Serve it this way up, or flip it again onto a second plate.

    2 minutes
  12. 12

    Slice and serve

    • Let it cool for another 15 minutes.
    • Cut it like a pizza into 8 slices.

    Wipe the knife between cuts for clean slices.

    15 minutes
Why it's lighter
  • Only 3 tbsp sugar. Ripe bananas add the sweetness.
  • No butter. Just 3 tbsp olive oil.
  • Half wholemeal flour for more fibre.
  • Regular milk instead of evaporated milk.
  • Dark chocolate chips.
Wet top? Lay a clean tea towel over the pot before closing the lid. It catches the drips.

Swaps

Use walnuts or raisins instead of chocolate chips. Or use all wholemeal flour. The cake will be a little denser.

Keeping

2 days in a closed box. 5 days in the fridge. Slices freeze well.
