Banana Choc-Chip Cake

A soft, moist banana cake baked right in the Philips Viva multicooker. It uses part wholemeal flour, only 3 spoons of sugar, a little olive oil and dark chocolate chips. Ripe bananas do most of the sweetening.

1 hr 1515 min prep, 50–60 min cooking
EasySkill level
3 tbspSugar in the whole cake
8Slices

Ingredients

Tick things off as you gather them.

Makes 1 cake (fills the multicooker pot), about 8 slices

Method

Two bowls, one multicooker pot.

  1. 1

    Prepare the pot

    Rub the oil for greasing all over the inside of the multicooker pot with a paper towel, covering the bottom and the sides. Sprinkle in about 1 spoon of flour, tilt the pot so the flour sticks to the oil, then tip out the extra. If you have baking paper, cut a circle to fit the bottom and lay it in. This helps the cake come out in one piece.

    3 minutes
  2. 2

    Mash the bananas

    Peel the bananas into a big bowl and mash them with a fork until mostly smooth. A few small lumps are fine. The riper (more brown spots) the bananas, the sweeter the cake.

    2 minutes
  3. 3

    Add the wet ingredients

    Crack in the eggs. Add the milk, olive oil, brown sugar (or honey) and vanilla. Whisk for about 1 minute until everything is well mixed.

    2 minutes
  4. 4

    Mix the dry ingredients

    In a second bowl, stir together the wholemeal flour, plain flour, baking powder, cinnamon and salt. Mixing these first spreads the baking powder evenly, so the cake rises evenly.

    1 minute
  5. 5

    Combine gently

    Tip the dry mix into the banana mix. Stir slowly with a spoon or spatula, only until you can't see any dry flour. Don't overmix, or the cake turns tough and heavy. Stir in most of the chocolate chips and keep a small handful for the top.

    2 minutes
  6. 6

    Fill the pot

    Pour the batter into the greased pot and smooth the top with the back of a spoon. Scatter the rest of the chips over the top. Tap the pot gently on the bench 2 or 3 times to knock out big air bubbles.

    1 minute
  7. 7

    Cook on the Cake setting

    Put the pot into the multicooker and close the lid. Choose the Cake (or Bake) program and set it to 50 minutes. If your model has no Cake program, use Rice/Cook and run it again when it finishes. Keep the lid closed for the first 40 minutes, or the cake may sink.

    50 minutes
  8. 8

    Check if it's done

    Push a skewer or thin knife into the middle of the cake. If it comes out clean or with a few dry crumbs, it's done. If there's wet batter on it, close the lid, cook for 10 more minutes, then check again.

    0–10 minutes
  9. 9

    Cool in the pot

    Lift the pot out of the cooker and leave the cake in it for 10 minutes. It firms up as it cools, so it's less likely to break when you turn it out.

    10 minutes
  10. 10

    Turn out and serve

    Run a knife around the edge. Put a plate upside down on top of the pot, hold both together and flip them over. The cake drops onto the plate. The bottom is golden and the top is pale, so serve whichever side up you like. Let it cool a little more before slicing.

    2 minutes
How it's lighter than the usual version
  • Sugar goes down from ⅓ cup to 3 tablespoons, because ripe bananas bring plenty of sweetness.
  • There's no butter. A small amount of olive or canola oil (3 tbsp) is used instead of ¼ cup.
  • Half the flour is wholemeal, which adds fibre.
  • Ordinary milk replaces evaporated milk.
  • The chips are dark chocolate (70% cocoa) instead of milk chocolate.
  • Baking powder is cut to 2 tsp. A full tablespoon is too much for 1 cup of flour and can taste bitter.
Soggy top? Steam drips back from the lid onto the cake. Next time, lay a clean tea towel or a double layer of paper towel across the top of the pot before closing the lid. It catches the drips.

Swaps

No chocolate chips? Use a handful of chopped walnuts or raisins. For more fibre, use all wholemeal flour. The cake will be a little denser.

Keeping

Store in a closed container on the bench for 2 days, or in the fridge for up to 5 days. Slices freeze well wrapped one by one.
