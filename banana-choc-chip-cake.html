Banana Choc-Chip Cake

A soft banana cake made in the multicooker. Little sugar, no butter, and the bananas do the sweetening.

1 hr 15Total time
EasySkill level
3 tbspSugar in the whole cake
8Slices

Ingredients

Tick them off as you go.

Makes 1 cake, about 8 slices

Method

Two bowls and the multicooker.

  1. 1

    Grease the pot

    Rub oil inside the pot. Sprinkle in a spoon of flour, shake it around, tip out the extra.

    2 minutes
  2. 2

    Mash the bananas

    Mash the bananas in a big bowl with a fork. Small lumps are fine.

    2 minutes
  3. 3

    Add the wet things

    Add the eggs, milk, oil, sugar and vanilla. Whisk well.

    2 minutes
  4. 4

    Mix the dry things

    In another bowl, mix the two flours, baking powder, cinnamon and salt.

    1 minute
  5. 5

    Combine gently

    Tip the dry mix into the wet mix. Stir only until no flour shows. Stir in most of the chocolate chips.

    2 minutes
  6. 6

    Fill the pot

    Pour the batter into the pot. Smooth the top and scatter the last chips on it.

    1 minute
  7. 7

    Cook

    Close the lid. Use the Cake setting for 50 minutes. Don't open the lid for the first 40.

    50 minutes
  8. 8

    Check it

    Poke a skewer in the middle. Clean? It's done. Wet? Cook 10 more minutes.

    0–10 minutes
  9. 9

    Cool

    Take the pot out. Leave the cake in it for 10 minutes.

    10 minutes
  10. 10

    Flip and serve

    Run a knife around the edge. Put a plate on top and flip it over. Slice when a bit cooler.

    2 minutes
Why it's lighter
  • Only 3 tbsp sugar. Ripe bananas add the sweetness.
  • No butter. Just 3 tbsp olive oil.
  • Half wholemeal flour for more fibre.
  • Regular milk instead of evaporated milk.
  • Dark chocolate chips.
Wet top? Lay a clean tea towel over the pot before closing the lid. It catches the drips.

Swaps

Use walnuts or raisins instead of chocolate chips. Or use all wholemeal flour. The cake will be a little denser.

Keeping

2 days in a closed box. 5 days in the fridge. Slices freeze well.
