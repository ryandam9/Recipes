Andhra Curd Rice

Soft rice mashed into light Greek yogurt with ginger, green chilli and cucumber, then finished with a crackling olive oil tempering of mustard seeds, dals and curry leaves. Cool, comforting and the classic way to end an Andhra meal.

35 minTotal time, plus cooling
EasySkill level
MildHeat, from green chilli

Servings
4

Method

Six steps, one pot for the rice and a small pan for the tempering.

  1. 1

    Cook the rice soft

    Rinse the rice well and cook it with the water until very soft, a little softer than you would for a curry. A pressure cooker takes 4 whistles; a pot with a lid takes about 20 minutes on low.

    About 20 minutes
  2. 2

    Mash and cool

    Mash the hot rice with the back of a ladle until creamy and broken down. Spread it out and let it cool until just lukewarm.

    About 10 minutes
  3. 3

    Loosen the yogurt

    Greek yogurt is thick, so whisk the light Greek yogurt with the milk and salt until smooth and pourable, like regular homemade curd.

    1 minute
  4. 4

    Mix it together

    Fold the yogurt into the rice with the grated ginger, green chillies and cucumber. It should be soft and slightly loose; add a splash more milk if it feels stiff.

    2 minutes
  5. 5

    Make the tempering

    Heat the olive oil in a small pan. Add the mustard seeds; when they pop, add the urad dal, chana dal and cumin and fry until the dals turn light golden. Add the dried red chillies, curry leaves and hing and fry for a few seconds.

    About 2 minutes
  6. 6

    Pour, mix and rest

    Pour the tempering over the rice and mix well. Finish with coriander leaves and pomegranate if using. Rest for 15 minutes, or chill, so the flavours settle.

    15 minutes resting
Don't add yogurt to hot rice. Heat makes the yogurt split and taste sour. Wait until the rice is just lukewarm. Curd rice also thickens as it sits, so stir in a little milk before serving leftovers.
