Baked Herb Falafel

Canned chickpeas blitzed with a big handful of parsley and coriander, onion, garlic and cumin, then brushed with olive oil and baked (or air-fried) until crisp outside and green and tender inside. All the crunch, none of the deep frying.

Method

Seven steps, one food processor and one tray.

  1. 1

    Heat the oven and dry the chickpeas

    Heat the oven to 200°C (180°C fan) and line a tray with baking paper. Rinse and drain the chickpeas, then roll them in a clean tea towel until really dry. Dry chickpeas are the secret to falafel that hold together.

    5 minutes
  2. 2

    Chop the herbs and aromatics

    Pulse the parsley, coriander, onion and garlic in a food processor until finely chopped. Scrape down the sides once or twice.

    1 minute
  3. 3

    Add the chickpeas and spices

    Add the chickpeas with the cumin, ground coriander, chilli flakes, salt, pepper and lemon juice. Pulse in short bursts until it looks like coarse, damp sand that clumps when pressed. Stop before it turns to hummus.

    1 minute
  4. 4

    Bind and chill

    Tip into a bowl and stir in the flour and baking powder. Squeeze a little in your hand: if it crumbles, add another tablespoon of flour. Cover and refrigerate so it firms up.

    15 minutes
  5. 5

    Make the sauce while it chills

    Whisk the light Greek yogurt, tahini, lemon juice, garlic and salt with a splash of water until smooth and spoonable. Keep it in the fridge.

    3 minutes
  6. 6

    Shape and oil

    Roll heaped tablespoons into balls, then flatten slightly into thick patties so they cook through evenly. Set on the tray, brush the tops with half the olive oil and scatter with sesame seeds if using.

    5 minutes
  7. 7

    Bake until crisp

    Bake for 12 minutes, flip, brush with the rest of the olive oil and bake another 10 to 12 minutes until deep golden. Air fryer: 190°C for 12 to 14 minutes, shaking halfway. Pan: a non-stick pan with 1 tbsp olive oil, 3 to 4 minutes a side over medium heat.

    About 25 minutes
Don't over-blend, and dry those chickpeas. Wet chickpeas or a smooth paste make falafel that spread and turn soft in the oven. Aim for a coarse, crumbly mix, and chill it so it shapes cleanly. Baked falafel firm up as they cool for a minute on the tray.
